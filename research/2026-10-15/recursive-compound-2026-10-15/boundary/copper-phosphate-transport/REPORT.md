# Proposed role 24: copper phosphate boundary, no target admission

The proposed complement is a flow-grown Cu(II)/phosphate wall, whose precise phase and stoichiometry remain unresolved. [Rauscher et al., PCCP 2018, DOI 10.1039/C7CP08282G](https://doi.org/10.1039/C7CP08282G), recovered as a [primary PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/4266/f614d4ecf67d12bdcdec0b53895675f454fa.pdf), reports Cu(OH)2 plus basic copper phosphate/silicate. A pure Cu3(PO4)2 formula would overstate the evidence.

At 0.30 M total phosphate and 0.71 mL/h pumping, pH 12.64 gives tubes, pH 9.08 short tubes, and pH 4.19 no precipitation (p.5767). Different sodium phosphate salts also change sodium/ionic conditions; this is not an isolated proton perturbation. The dimensionless geometric growth/pump slope is 1.89 +/- 0.05 (p.5768). Matched comparisons give 2.0 +/- 0.2 for silicate and 2.1 +/- 0.2 for phosphate (p.5769). Reported uncertainty types are unspecified. Table 2 supplies five linear-growth observations, retained in linear-growth.csv. Figure 4 specifies 1.0 M silicate for its microscopy arm; that value is not automatically assigned to other experiments.

The source supports inorganic boundary formation and a proposed osmotic contribution in its own apparatus. It supplies no active-POM retention or acidic survival test. The pH 4.19 formation failure neither measures dissolution of preformed walls nor tests the separate parent-Mo36 assembly medium near pH 1.7. Target medium, identity and function remain unverified. Root visually checked printed pp.5767-5770 after bounded acquisition; the paper carries a CC BY 3.0 notice. Organic pump tubing is apparatus, not evidence for an organic active wall.

Disposition pending independent review: assess this distinct boundary proposition, reject direct transfer of the reported alkaline formation recipe as an established target-compatible boundary, and admit no integrated complement. No physical test, target fit, rate calibration or held-out target validation was performed. The geometric observation's inference limits are derived in model-observable.md. The next chemistry priority remains the existing acidic zirconia comparator's joint identity/competence assay rather than assigning a permeability to this growth slope.
