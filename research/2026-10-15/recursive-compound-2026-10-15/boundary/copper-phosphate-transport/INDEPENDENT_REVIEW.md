## Balance and identifiability

With Vl=Vg−Vw, differentiating the definition and inserting dVl/dt=Qp+Qm−Qt+Sv yields exactly dVg/dt=Qp+Qm−Qt+Sv+dVw/dt. Every term is volume/time, and division by nonzeroQp yields a dimensionless ratio. Positive inwardQm and outwardQt give the correct signs. Sv must account for liquid volume consumed/created by wall reaction and mixing without double counting solid volume. Moving-tip capture and losses must be assigned consistently to the control volume; they are not measured by the five length-growth rows.

Required clarification1: define pressure jumps for the illustrative constitutive law. With inward-positiveQm, DeltaP=Poutside−Pinside and DeltaPi=pioutside−piinside make Qm=A*Lp*(DeltaP−sigma*DeltaPi) consistent with positive inward osmosis when inside osmotic pressure is greater. Lp dimensions are length/(time*pressure), A area, sigma dimensionless. Opposite jump definitions would require opposite signs. Current model text leaves jumps undefined; this is a notation gap, not an experimentally identified sign error.

Required clarification2: reported regression slope1.89 is not automatically each observation's ratio(dVg/dt)/Qp. For dVg/dt=a+bQp the ratio is b+a/Qp. A zero intercept cannot be silently imposed. The source calls1.89 an average slope; Fig2 shows a fitted line and does not establish a fixedzero-intercept fit. The balance applies to pointwise rate ratios, and a slope constrains derivatives across runs only with corresponding conditions. The conclusion that it cannot identify selectivity is sound either way.

Exact no-target counterexample: keep all wall, solvent and background-electrolyte processes the same, and set target initial inventory, target feed and target-producing source terms tozero. Any target-specific transport coefficientP and first-order inactivationk multiply zero target concentration/gradient. Thus target stayszero and geometric observations stay identical for P=0, finiteP or an ideal unrestricted-passage limit. This is an admissible family of indistinguishable models; no target-selectivity assumption is inferred or required. General models that arbitrarily tie a target parameter to background transport need not have this invariance, but such an unvalidated constitutive tie would not identify target transport from this experiment. The claim is lack of identification, not zero inactivation or zero passage in a later target-containing assay.
