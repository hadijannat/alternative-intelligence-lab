# Acidic nanofiltration: stronger boundary evidence, no target admission

The existing B2 proposition now has a concrete membrane architecture: tetragonal ZrO2 above a 3 mol% yttria-stabilized zirconia interlayer and alpha-alumina support. It differs from the earlier monoclinic powder adsorption experiment. [Van Gestel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.memsci.2006.07.020) exposed this architecture successively to pH 2, 13 and 1 for four weeks each. Corrosion exposure used 5–7 bar; separate PEG characterization used 10 bar. Printed cutoff annotations span 200–290, and permeability changed from 2.28 to 2.35 L h^-1 m^-2 bar^-1. Missing uncertainty prevents an improvement claim. These are sequential observations, without demonstrated independent replication. Acid was nitric, whereas the parent baseline uses chloride. Organic preparation additives and PEG assays are disclosed; residual carbon was not quantified. No POM or boundary renewal was tested.

[Roy Chowdhury et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.200501021) report 97% retention for aqueous sodium zinc-tungstate POM through a different, gamma-alumina membrane. Concentration came from zinc analysis after destroying the sample, so molecular survival remains unresolved. Six catalytic recycling runs belong to the organic-ammonium/toluene arm. The paper also leaves a 4.0/4.3-nm pore-radius discrepancy and inconsistent descriptions of purification. Smaller precursor retention was unmeasured. The 97% value is retained as the reported endpoint, without conversion to recovered mass or active-target retention.

Decision: refine the existing zirconia proposition for a measured same-medium parent/target test; add no candidate role or verified complement. Required observations are intact identity and recoverable seed activity on both sides, precursor/proton flux, and adsorption/extraction controls. PEG cutoff cannot predict charged-cluster selectivity. Combining these two papers supplies no integrated function, inheritance or held-out target validation.
