# Exact metal-accounting counterexample

This is a formal stoichiometric counterexample, not a proposed synthesis, measured equilibrium or claim that these species coexist at the target conditions. The beta-VMo7 source supplies a characterized competing cluster; the following calculation asks what bulk elemental measurements alone can distinguish.

One beta-[VMo7O26]5- unit plus 27 molybdate units and one tungstate unit contains exactly Mo34 W1 V1, the same metal counts as one nominal target. The two assignments therefore give identical total Mo/W/V signals at matched inventory, even though the first contains zero target molecules. Their formal atom/charge bookkeeping can be written

`[VMo7O26]5- + 27[MoO4]2- + [WO4]2- + 52H+ = [Mo34WVO112(H2O)16]9- + 10H2O`.

Both sides contain Mo34, W1, V1, O138 and H52, with charge -9. The equality denotes conservation only; it gives no reaction direction, equilibrium constant, kinetic rate, admissible preparation or operating pH. Acid/base/speciation constraints may disallow an assignment in a particular measured medium, but those constraints are additional evidence absent from an elemental total. The target's 16 written waters are counted as part of its nominal formula, without asserting their physical coordination.

Consequently, even exact recovery of the target's metal ratio does not identify an intact target fraction. Bulk vanadium and molybdenum spectra in the same tube also require assignments that distinguish coexisting species from the same mixed-metal molecule. A discriminating assay must establish species identity and metal co-association, then recover competence in the identified fraction, with matched competing-anion and free-feed controls. This counterexample calibrates no parameter and contributes zero complement admissions.
