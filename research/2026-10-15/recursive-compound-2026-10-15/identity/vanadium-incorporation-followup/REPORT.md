# V(V) incorporation in ordinary Mo36: bounded source follow-up

Date: 2026-10-16 (Europe/Berlin). Scope: six targeted searches and four distinct new primary-source/deposit URLs. Existing mixed-addenda, novelty, Sánchez Sánchez and Dieterle records were inspected first. No structure calculations or new candidate classifications were performed.

**Outcome:** no isolated/refined V(V) site in ordinary Krebs Mo36, Mo36-specific solution 51V NMR/EXAFS assignment, or simultaneous Mo34WV identity was found. A newly acquired primary thesis supplies a useful constraint: vanadium precursor identity changes the observed Mo/V assembly. This challenges transferring V(IV)-feed observations to the exact V(V) target; it does not show that the target is thermodynamically impossible. The nominal `[MoVI34WVI VV O112(H2O)16]9−` hypothesis remains unchanged and unverified.

## New measured constraint

**Sabrina Jung, TU Berlin doctoral thesis, 2018**, *Speciation of molybdenum- and vanadium-based polyoxometalate species in aqueous medium and gas-phase and its consequences for M1 structured MoV oxide synthesis*. [Primary deposited PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2622015/component/file_2639730/content). Defense: 19 July 2018; 158 PDF pages. Retained as `raw/jung_sabrina.pdf`, SHA256 `1f9b2960feb8866dd2067cb39ac52e23b49cab73196b4d413089e74d105c3eb6`.

Chapter 5 compares **VOSO4 and NH4VO3 feeds**, formally V(IV) and V(V), at **Mo:V = 4:1** and total metal concentrations **250, 43.75 and 8.75 mM**. These are precursor labels, not measured Mo36-bound valences. Vanadyl solutions show Mo72V30-associated Raman signals; their Keplerate UV–visible signature appears at 250 mM and disappears on dilution. Metavanadate solutions show octamolybdate-like signals without that UV–visible signature above 350 nm. The salts also change sulfate/ammonium inputs: this does not isolate oxidation state as the sole cause.

Locators: printed pp. 89–92, Figures 5.1–5.2 and Tables 5.1–5.2 (PDF pp. 107–110). Table 5.1 reports 870 cm−1 in the vanadyl series, assigned to Mo72V30; the metavanadate series reports 915, 950, 968 and 994 cm−1. The assignments are not refined molecular occupancies.

**Source-quality limits:** experimental §2.1.3 gives pH 2.8 (printed p.22/PDF p.40), while §5.1.1 gives approximately 4.2 (p.89/PDF p.107). Figure 5.1's top/bottom caption conflicts with the surrounding prose. No correction was imposed. ESI-MS species abundances are not accepted as solution mole fractions; the source itself cautions against that inference (p.101/PDF p.119).

## Existing tentative incorporation evidence has an oxidation-state gap

The retained [Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2012 manuscript/SI](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1849723/component/file_1854326/content), DOI [10.1002/anie.201200746](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201200746), uses **VOSO4·5H2O**, formally V(IV), in SI §§1.1–1.2. SI §4.2, printed p.12, attributes Raman shifts only tentatively to partial V substitution in both a nanocrystalline solid and **Nb-containing `{Mo36−xNbxO112}` solution anions**. It does not determine cluster-bound V oxidation state, substitution count or site occupancy. Table S1's highest-oxidation-state treatment is a yield-calculation assumption, not a valence measurement.

Dieterle's retained 2001 thesis, §5.2.7.1, pp.163–165, concerns mixed Mo/V/W solutions and gives tentative species assignments. Its separately assigned V(V)-compatible species cannot be reinterpreted as a measured V(V) atom in Mo36. These records remain negative/ambiguous evidence; their original results were not altered.

## Species-resolved NMR lead and its limit

**Howarth, Pettersson and Andersson (1991)**, [*Aqueous molybdovanadates at high Mo:V ratio*](https://doi.org/10.1039/DT9910001799), pp.1799–1812. The [author-institution record](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/22597/) reports quantitative 51V NMR plus potentiometry at 25 °C in 0.6 mol dm−3 Na(Cl), with NMR studies covering pH 1.4–7. Parallel 17O NMR supports several smaller Mo/V anions, including β-[Mo7VO26]5−. No ordinary Mo36 species is identified in the inspected abstract. This is a useful competing-species reference, not a target occupancy dataset. Full-text access failed; chemical shifts, formation constants, uncertainties and complete concentration ranges remain null.

A [2018 conference abstract hosted by the University of Vienna](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/synthesis-and-speciation-of-mixed-addenda-vanadium-substituted-po/) reports 51V NMR studies of mixed solutions, but its named isolated products are fluoride/peroxide vanadium species. It supplies no Mo36 formula, occupancy or numerical spectrum and is not promoted to that role.

## What changes in the next identity test

The defensible next hypothesis is **competition between parent Mo36, separate vanadium species and alternative Mo/V assemblies**, with intact V(V)-bearing Mo36 as an unverified branch. A model that forces every added V into a retained Mo36 site lacks support. The new measurements do not justify switching the target to V(IV), extrapolating their 4:1 Mo:V mixture to the target's 34:1 ratio, or importing the conflicted pH values as fitted conditions. No target-specific equilibrium constant or retention parameter was obtained.

A useful falsifier is condition-specific: if a validated species-resolved measurement accounts for V in separate vanadate/smaller Mo–V species while the Mo36 population remains the parent, with sufficient sensitivity to exclude the required substituted fraction, then persistent V(V) occupancy is rejected **for those conditions and that detection limit**. A missing 51V signal alone does not meet this test. Acceptance requires an assigned V-bearing molecular species and an independent Mo36-framework identity, followed by site/valence evidence; total elemental V, a Raman shift or a mixed-metal mass envelope alone cannot supply all three.

Required missing evidence remains: V occupancy and uncertainty; V oxidation state; W/V coexistence in the same ordinary Mo36 molecule; counterions/protonation/water inventory; and solution persistence. All are null in `evidence.json`. The search cap and access failures are preserved in `acquisition.json`; a negative search establishes neither novelty nor impossibility.
