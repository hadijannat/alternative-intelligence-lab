# Manganese regeneration: transfer constraints

**No verified complement is added.** This extends the existing manganese-renewal evidence rather than adding another candidate-role count. Li et al., [Nature Sustainability (2025), 10.1038/s41893-025-01665-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-025-01665-y), studies thermally prepared MnO2 on an FTO electrode. Its proposed recovery route uses Mn(VII)/Mn(II) comproportionation to Mn(III), followed by anodic reincorporation. The acquired main article and supplement are one retrospective evidence family.

The operating electrolyte is pH2 with sulfate and1M phosphate. Figure3 uses two-electrode cell voltages of1.68/3.00V, with180/8minute stages. The nominal high-voltage fraction is8/188=4.255%; the text separately reports approximately4.5%. The reported greater-than2100hour duration therefore does not describe continuous250mA/cm2 operation. QCM uses300/20second stages: its reported loss/gain slopes belong to that different protocol and cannot establish long-cycle mass balance.

Supplement FigureS4 (PDFp6) reports no Mn(III) spectroscopic signature within1hour for100microM Mn(II)+25microM Mn(VII) atpH2 in sulfate without phosphate. No detection limit is supplied. FigureS6 (PDFp8) shows altered catalytic response with phosphate. FigureS7 (PDFp9) labels a reference gamma-MnO2 with100%pyrolusite; this audit does not resolve that phase terminology. Numerical source data are available by author request; none was requested or acquired.

These observations do not establish phosphate-free regeneration; the tested phosphate-free solution showed no detectable Mn(III) signature over1hour. This was not a deposition or catalytic-function assay. Nondetection does not establish zero reaction, nor does a spectroscopic intermediate alone establish useful regeneration. The phosphate-rich experiment cannot inherit Mo34WV compatibility from a shared pH value. The target's speciation and useful function in that medium are unmeasured.

Electrode mass recovery and total current do not partition catalytic function between remaining founders, reorganized founders and redeposited material. No positive lower bound on newly competent material follows. A discriminating next assay would transfer measured dissolved products to a blank receiver electrode, validate exclusion of founder particles, deposit and wash, then measure oxygen production in a common fresh electrolyte. Blank, feed-only, no-regeneration-transfer and carryover controls would bound alternative explanations. This is a proposed measurement, not an executed result; external-feed tracing would additionally be needed to distinguish reconstructed founder atoms from new atoms.

The prospective receiver assay addresses only active reconstruction. Target identity, same-medium operation, inherited variants and adaptive resource control remain separate unmet requirements. No rate fitting, physical experiment or held-out target validation was performed.
