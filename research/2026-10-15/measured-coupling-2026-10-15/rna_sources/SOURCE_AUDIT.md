# Primary-source audit: chemically coupled RNA systems

Two primary experimental systems were inspected on 2026-10-15. The selected calibration source is [Salibi, Peter, Schwille and Mutschler, Nature Communications14,1222(2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-36940-z), which reports reaction activity, physical content loss, and repeated serial transfer for one POPC/R3C preparation. It supplies a public3.15MB source workbook with replicate values. [Ameta et al., Communications Chemistry6,91(2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-023-00887-5) supplies useful corroborating compartment chemistry and catalytic networks, but no public numerical raw data table; those data are available from the authors upon reasonable request. No author contact was made.

## Salibi: same preparation, distinct evidence layers

The R3C GUV reaction buffer is50mM EPPS pH8.5,20mM MgCl2,900mM sucrose internally and900mM glucose externally. RNA activity is read after60min at42C. Each PAGE FT cycle is5min at−80C and5min at room temperature, with three FT cycles per serial generation. Initial GUV populations separately contain F1(0,1,or2uM), Hyper-A10uM plus Cy5-A2.5uM, and B15uM; they are mixed1:1:1. At later generations, half the old sample is combined with an equal volume of fresh A/B substrate GUVs(1:1). Thus feed-average A/R/B concentrations are5/1.25/7.5uM before the final1:1 replacement, unlike the initial three-population averages10/3,2.5/3,5uM.

The paper estimates about45% content loss over three FT cycles from source gels. It does not identify per-species permeability, vesicle survival, exact changing interior volume, or confidence limits for that number. Modeling it as one common concentration-retention factor is an assumption. Small oligonucleotide fluorescence distributions inS9 are a different probe preparation and cannot supply an interchangeable exact R3C leakage rate.

Hyper-A is a52nt fragment and B is a14nt5-prime-triphosphate fragment. They ligate to active66nt F1. The fluorescent Cy5-A carries an inactivating G38A mutation; its ligated product Cy5-F1 is catalytically inactive. The PAGE observer therefore directly measures inactive reporter product, while active catalyst regeneration is inferred through related chemistry and persistence. A valid model must keep these products separate, include reporter carryover, and consume substrates for both products. The fluorescence yield is not an absolute concentration of active catalyst.

S13c kinetics use the same buffer, sugar and42C as the GUV interior, but have no membrane. Its F1 series contains5uM Cy5-A,7.5uM B and0.1–1uM F1, without Hyper-A; its Hyper-A series contains5uM Cy5-A,7.5uM B and1–2.5uM Hyper-A, without initial F1. Transferring fitted rates to vesicles is a same-buffer extrapolation with unmeasured membrane/crowding effects. S12 andS15 omit sucrose in their methods, so they are excluded from quantitative shared-rate calibration. S18 also differs in sugar reporting and uses10min freezing+10min thawing rather than the main GUV5+5min cycle; it supports only a qualitative caveat that frozen/thaw chemistry is not exactly zero.

The observed serial assay mixes old and new GUV populations and transfers RNA horizontally. It does not track mother/daughter compartments, select a heritable fitness phenotype, demonstrate sequence copying, or produce autonomous compartment growth and division. The article explicitly says the prepared-fragment ligase cannot evolve under its tested conditions or confer compartment fitness. Functional vertical inheritance remains unidentifiable.

## Ameta: useful comparator, incompatible imported rates

The preparation uses5mM spermine,12.5mM high-MW PAA(>4MDa),25mM Tris-HCl(pH8 stock) and AZ buffer30mM EPPS/50mM MgCl2(pH7 stock), incubated48C. A separately measured final mixed pH is not reported; `conditions.json` retains the distinction. Four-fragment W/X/Y/Z assembly uses0.75uM each; network experiments use0.5uM WXY per node with equivalent Z and radioactive tracer. The macrophase removes bulk aqueous solution before reaction; dispersed droplets retain surrounding medium. These are different transport geometries.

The system makes ~200nt Azoarcus ribozymes by covalent assembly of prepared fragments. Noncovalent fragment complexes are themselves active and dominate parts of the initial assembly; apparent sigmoidal growth alone cannot prove autonomous self-copying. Seed dependence and cross-catalytic assays support real catalytic function. Network composition is protected on a30min perturbation timescale but changes by4h. This is transient compositional robustness, not measured generation-to-generation inheritance.

FRAP used a30nt fluorescent DNA probe, reporting droplet recovery21.3±3.0s and macrophase172.5±4.0s. Those recovery times mix geometry and transport and cannot be inserted as full-lengthRNA decay or leakage rates. Lipid-coated coacervates provide another boundary condition. SI TableS2 has author model parameters as an embedded image; the raw numerical trajectories are not publicly supplied. We retain it for audit rather than importing uncertain graphical coefficients into the unrelated R3C model.

## Recommendation

Use Salibi as the quantitative joint-condition propagation test, retaining effective-parameter uncertainty and prediction failures. Use Ameta to motivate a future measured network-identity experiment, with its own matched transport and transfer measurements. Neither source supports a completed living entity or demonstrated functional parent-offspring inheritance. Do not splice rates across these systems.
