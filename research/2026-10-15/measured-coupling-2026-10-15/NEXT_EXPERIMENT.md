# Measurements that would resolve the coupled question

This is a proposed measurement design, not an executed physical experiment or a synthesis protocol. The two numerical laboratories retain their own source chemistry. They are alternatives, not ingredients of one mixed recipe.

## The missing joint observation

The decisive observation is newly generated, catalytically active material in an identified descendant compartment, linked to its parent's functional state, under the same conditions in which the compartment survives. Bulk product yield, turbidity, tracer mobility and serial population propagation each leave part of this observation missing.

For every parent and sampled descendant, measure four quantities separately: original catalyst remaining, newly formed catalyst, catalytic activity, and compartment identity/volume. Use an independently validated molecular label or isotope assay to distinguish old from new material; a fluorescent product signal alone is insufficient if the label changes catalytic activity. Validate recovery and detection limits in each phase. Report missing and lost compartments as outcomes.

## Fueled droplet branch

Start from one complete published formulation and one catalyst identity. The C3 formation and turbidity assays of [Soria-Carrera et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chempr.2025.102816) share nominal conditions, but catalytic retention and daughter transmission are unmeasured. The KR3 competition/refueling experiment is a separate formulation with unresolved stock/final concentration descriptions. Resolve that recipe before a quantitative replication; do not import its selectivity into the C3 rate fit.

Measure active catalyst in both dense and dilute phases during production, dissolution and refueling. Measure phase volumes concurrently. Distinguish retention from uptake of newly supplied catalyst and from activity in the exterior. Internal fluorescence recovery does not supply this measurement. Track whether a division/fragmentation event actually occurs and which daughter receives which material; an externally imposed volume transfer should be recorded as such.

Measure catalytic activity of isolated new product with fresh substrate under a common assay condition. Inactive-product, no-regeneration, no-fuel, passive-retention and matched fresh-initiation controls should receive the same handling and feed. A shared medium or pooled transfer control tests whether an apparently inherited function is maintained by exchange through the environment.

## RNA vesicle branch

Replicate the [Salibi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-36940-z) source conditions as one package. Directly quantify active F1 and inactive Cy5-F1, along with substrates, in the compartment and exterior. Estimate species-specific recovery through freeze–thaw and dilution instead of treating the approximate aggregate loss as a measured permeability. Test kinetics inside GUVs because matching the buffer to a bulk assay does not validate membrane or concentration effects.

The source's prepared fragments, fresh vesicles, freeze–thaw schedule and warm incubation are external inputs. Track them explicitly. To test vertical inheritance, introduce independently distinguishable parent functional states and track actual parent–daughter compartments. Existing bulk serial-transfer data primarily test population propagation through horizontal exchange; relabeling compartments cannot turn that into reproduction.

## Decision rules to register before new data

1. **Renewal:** New material must have independently measured catalytic activity. Quantify activity per amount, measurement error and assay interference. A retained old catalyst or inactive reporter increase does not meet this rule.
2. **Retention with renewal:** Determine the joint distribution of production, loss and descendant transfer in the same condition and cohort. Do not multiply means taken from separate systems. For a transferred lineage, report newly functional catalyst delivered to the next stage per starting functional catalyst, with feed and dilution accounted for. A factor exceeding one is a useful replacement screen where repeated amplification is required; it is not a definition of life.
3. **Functional inheritance:** Parent identity or controlled parent functional state must predict descendant function in a common environment after conditioning on founder carryover and externally supplied material. Compare with randomized parent assignments and pooled exchange while matching amounts. A single chemical species retained after transfer establishes persistence of that function at most; it cannot establish transmission of variant information.
4. **Founder exclusion:** Propagate an experimentally measured upper bound for old functional material through each observed loss/transfer. Require descendant activity above the corresponding old-material-only bound, and verify that the excess is due to new active material. Numerical removal of a founder tag is not a physical separation method.
5. **Compatibility:** Require the renewal, retention and functional transmission outcomes together in the same tested formulation. Count failures, dissolution and unobserved descendants. Keep the complete dataset; specify independent replicates and analytical detection limits before collecting outcomes.

No sample-size or success-probability claim is supplied here: the necessary variance, lineage recovery and activity-detection measurements are not available. Use a pilot to estimate those nuisance quantities, then freeze the confirmatory design. Adaptive behavior would be a later experiment with matched direct-response and memory-erasure controls; neither current chemistry dataset measures intelligence.
