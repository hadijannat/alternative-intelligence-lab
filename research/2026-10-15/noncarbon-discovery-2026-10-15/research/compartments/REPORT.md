# Carbon-free compartments: source audit and candidate constraints

Audit date: 2026-10-15. This is a bounded literature and data-availability investigation. No novel compound, living system, intelligent behavior, or functional inheritance was demonstrated. Candidate compositions below are testable architectures, not predictions that they will live.

The strongest admitted compartment lead is an iron hydroxide/sulfide precipitation membrane supplied by inorganic feeds. Published POM iCHELLs and the best-known silica colloidosome growth/division systems require organic components. Their useful physical demonstrations remain valid, but they cannot establish strict carbon-free construction.

## What the primary sources establish

**Iron hydroxide/sulfide gardens.** Barge et al. measured voltage and current across precipitated membranes. The archived supplementary Table 1 includes an organic-free-feed row: 25 mM FeCl2 + 75 mM FeCl3 facing 100 mM NaOH + 50 mM Na2S; listed maxima are 1.4 mA and 1.07 V. These are separate maxima, so their product is not measured power. Other rows contain methanol or dialysis supports and must remain separate. The experiment supports driven precipitation and electrical output; it does not show that output maintains or reproduces the compartment. Phase identity requires measurement rather than the blanket label “FeS.” [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201501663); [author archive](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/e0cz9-bgc19).

**Iron/silicate gardens.** Glaab et al. monitored inner/outer ion concentrations, pH, and electrochemical potentials after forming tubes from iron chlorides and sodium silicate. The external pH was approximately 11.2. Iron(II) examples retained approximately 70 mV after 24 h and 40 mV after 45 h, but no loaded-current history was established. Wall analysis did not establish a continuous silica component in the iron(II) case. Thus a nominal iron–silicate recipe does not identify a uniform silica membrane, and a decaying gradient does not establish autonomous maintenance. [Open primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1039/C6CP02107G).

**POM iCHELLs.** Cooper et al. explicitly describe hybrid membranes formed by exchanging small counterions on POMs for organic or coordination-complex cations. Examples include [PW12O40]3− or [PMo12O40]3− with phenanthridinium derivatives or Ru(bipyridyl) complexes. Reported functions include repair upon reactant contact, differential passage of ammonium ions, nested compartments, and peroxide diffusion. Fission was future work in that paper. Replacing its organic cations with Na+, NH4+, or an arbitrary inorganic cation is an untested material substitution, not an established carbon-free iCHELL. [Primary author PDF](https://www.chem.gla.ac.uk/cronin/images/pubs/Cooper2011AngewChem.pdf).

**iCHELL energy conversion.** The later photochemical hydrogen-evolution system uses POM anions together with methyl viologen, Ru(bpy)3, triethanolamine, and platinum. Its energy-conversion result depends on multiple carbon-containing components. It cannot be imported as a carbon-free metabolism. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6348376/).

**Silica compartments.** The 2013 electrostatically gated membrane uses crosslinking and a covalently grafted pH-responsive copolymer. The 2014 growth/division demonstration relies on organosilane-mediated methanol formation in silica-stabilized droplets. Both are valuable comparators; neither demonstrates an entirely inorganic self-reproducing membrane. An unmodified silica gel or porous glass remains a distinct, simpler confinement hypothesis whose retention and repair must be measured. [2013 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.1644); [2014 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.201400639).

**Clay inheritance.** The original Weiss claim was challenged directly by Arrhenius, Cairns-Smith, Hartman, Miller, and Orgel in 1986 because the disclosed methods did not permit repetition of the key replication experiments. The author-deposited letter was inspected; it is stronger evidence of this limitation than later retellings. Modern direct observations of montmorillonite show ion-dependent curvature, exfoliation, and restacking, which are physical reorganization, not a demonstrated hereditary code. [1986 primary letter, author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/250411536_Bemerkungen_zum_Aufsatz_Replikation_und_Evolution_in_anorganischen_Systemen_vonArmin_Weiss); [letter DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.198606581); [2020 primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09295).

## Compatibility limits

The 2023 POM speciation atlas measured ten POMs in buffered aqueous environments immediately and after 24 h at 37 °C. Identity depends on pH, buffer, time, and counterions. PW12 hydrolyzed under the tested conditions; the highly charged Preyssler P5W30 was comparatively persistent. Many buffers were organic; only relevant inorganic-medium subsets can constrain a strict carbon-free proposal. The raw NMR archive is identified below. These measurements do not supply transport coefficients through mineral walls. [Primary atlas](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi0814).

**Inference:** direct contact between an acidic Mo/W POM subsystem and an alkaline growing garden must be treated as a speciation experiment. Neutralization, precipitation, adsorption, redox changes, and leakage may destroy the intended active species even while total Mo/W remains retained. This cannot be represented as simple conserved “catalyst concentration.” Acidic, sulfide-containing and oxidizing domains likewise require measured phase and redox compatibility. A chemically distinct compartment on each side of a separator is a candidate architecture, not evidence of successful integration.

No neutral/alkaline compatibility of a VOx memory material is established by these sources. A solid-state hysteresis or switching result must be remeasured under the exact aqueous electrolyte, pH, oxygen exposure, and electrical history. The device substrate/electrodes and any organics are part of the dependency audit.

## Three candidate architectures

| ID | Concrete architecture | Carbon status and evidence class | Main falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| NC-COMP-1 | Inorganic-feed iron hydroxide/sulfide precipitation membrane separating iron-chloride and alkaline sulfide streams | Feed-level carbon-free compartment/electrical subsystem is experimentally supported. No claimed pure compound or self-reproduction. Highest-priority compartment baseline. | Under matched precipitation and flow, catalytic activity makes no difference to maintained selectivity, repair, or useful output. |
| NC-COMP-2 | Preformed unmodified SiO2 porous support containing a separately characterized acidic Mo/W POM pool; H+/Na+/K+ counterions only | Proposed compatibility/retention assay. No organic linker or iCHELL cation assumed. No claim that confinement, growth, or copying already works. | Dissolved POM activity washes out or changes speciation while total metal remains adsorbed; no catalytic advantage over unconfined controls. |
| NC-COMP-3 | Na/Li-exchanged montmorillonite hydrated network adjacent to a separately characterized VOx surface | Carbon-free mineral scaffold is plausible; dynamic clay structure is measured. Closed compartments, integrated memory, repair, and inheritance remain unverified. Lowest-priority integration lead. | Apparent memory is explained by electrolyte equilibration or irreversible aging and disappears after matched-state resetting. |

These candidates are neither a novelty claim nor a ranking of the probability of life. NC-COMP-2 and NC-COMP-3 deliberately expose missing measurements before any rate fitting. Mineral formulas alone do not specify active phases, porosity, hydration, or counterion distributions.

## Discriminating measurements and held-out design

1. Measure **active species, not elemental retention**: total metal balance plus species-resolved spectroscopy and a standardized activity assay on inside, outside, and wall-associated fractions. Count adsorbed inactive material separately.
2. Measure permeability for feedstock, active catalyst, inactive product, and proton/charge transport independently. Fit species-specific fluxes with both adsorption and desorption; include ionic strength, pH, concentration, and wall age. No transport rate is borrowed from a different garden chemistry.
3. Record synchronized voltage and current under known load with pressure, flow, temperature, and material balance. Open-circuit voltage alone cannot establish available work or maintenance energy.
4. After a controlled perturbation, compare recovery of selectivity and catalytic function with an abiotic precipitation control at matched reagent activities. New wall mass or repeated osmotic rupture alone is not reproduction.
5. If fragments or daughter compartments are produced, track multiple founders through serial growth with source-material carryover accounting. Compare specific catalytic function against no-seed, inactive-seed, and composition-matched seed controls. Persistent morphology or total metal is insufficient.
6. Freeze a measured-mechanism model before testing complete held-out runs or entire chemical conditions. Withhold whole concentration/flow/age combinations, not adjacent frames from the same movie. Retain all failures and comparison against passive diffusion–precipitation models. These are proposed experiments; no held-out validation was executed in this subtask.

## Verification and completion

Ownership was restricted to this directory. Existing laboratories were not edited. Two new primary PDFs and extracted text are retained under `raw/`; `sources.json` records source type, access, and limits; `candidate_constraints.csv` records the three candidate gates. A machine check validates unique IDs, source references, required fields, and local SHA256 values.

Skipped: wet synthesis, parameter fitting, large image archives, raw NMR reprocessing, and a global novelty search. Main risk: combining individually supported mineral subsystems can change their chemistry and erase the function being combined.
