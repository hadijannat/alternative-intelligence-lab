## Chemistry candidates and observer

Concentrations are micromolar, time minutes. States are Hyper-A (A), accessible Cy5-A (R), B, active F1 (F), inactive Cy5-F1 (P), inactive ablation product (D), and inaccessible Cy5-A (I). Every active ligation consumes A+B and creates F; reporter ligation consumes R+B and creates P. P and I never catalyse. Disabling regeneration sends active ligation product to D, preserving stoichiometry and founder catalysis. Three conserved moieties are A+F+D, R+P+I, and B+F+P+D. The PAGE observer is 100 P/(R+P+I), summed by interior volume across cohorts.

Four reduced binding/assembly hypotheses use sB=B/(KB+B), va=kF F A sB+kH A² sB², and vr=kF F R sB+h kH A R sB². h is fixed to 0 or 1. The latter shared-coefficient route tests direct Hyper-A-assisted reporter ligation. SI13b explicitly proposes Hyper-A-catalysed reporter ligation; exact reaction order, transient assembly and coefficient equality remain unmeasured. In contrast, h=0 requires Hyper-A to create F before reporter ligation. Each route is evaluated with q fixed to 1 or a fitted common accessible fraction q: initially R=q Rtotal, I=(1-q)Rtotal. This is a conditional inaccessible-reporter reservoir hypothesis, not measured damage or an established detector ceiling. It predicts assay behavior and preserves the hidden reservoir explicitly. No condition-specific plateau is allowed. Reusing bulk q inside GUVs is an additional unmeasured extrapolation.

kF and kH units are µM^-1 min^-1; KB is µM. Rates are effective coarse laws, not elementary reaction mechanisms. In particular A² sB² approximates association of two A/B complexes. No product inhibition is retained in the new candidates because the previous Ki reached its upper bound. The old three-parameter model va=k F A B/H+kb A²B², vr=k F R B/H, H=1+(F+P+D)/Ki is refit and cross-validated with exactly the same budget as a comparator. No RNA degradation, unmeasured temperature-rate extrapolation, or per-condition catalytic efficiency is fitted.

## Compartment events and independent transport comparison

Retain original chemical inventories and equal-volume cohort assumption: seed, A/R, and B populations each weigh one-third, containing seed0/1/2, A10/Rtotal2.5, or B15 µM respectively. Each generation has three FT events then 60 minutes at42°C. Generation2 onward retains half old sample and adds fresh A/R and B cohorts each weight0.25. No division or offspring formation is simulated.

Compare two single-parameter FT closures without changing selected chemistry: fractional exchange maps every cohort x to (1-m)x+m mean(x); contact exchange selects fraction m of every cohort into one new mixed cohort with total weight m and leaves fraction1-m uncontacted. Both conserve weighted chemical amounts before loss. Apply per-event retention r=(0.55)^(1/3) to every species by default; this is the paper's approximate aggregate loss estimate, not measured active-catalyst retention. Fit each m on only original Figure2 zero/1µM seed conditions, FT1/2/3 (18 replicate observations). No transport model is selected using historical benchmarks; report both with full results.

With all fitting complete, report Figure2 2µM and Figure3 as historical development benchmarks against frozen original predictions. Preserve old pragmatic screen RMSE<=2pp and maximum absolute group-mean error<=4pp; this is adequacy of these observations, never functional inheritance. Fixed controls: no regeneration and no exchange.
