# Contact-directed sodium-chlorate state transfer: source audit

## Decision

The acquired native observations support a useful **candidate experiment for contact-triggered transfer of a crystal state**. They do not establish founder-bounded functional inheritance. The candidate material is documented NaClO3, with a fabricated plasmonic Au substrate and externally directed laser contact. It is not a verified complement to Mo34WV and closes no integrated requirement.

The exact main source is [Niinomi et al. 2020, DOI 10.1021/acs.cgd.0c00693](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.cgd.0c00693). The public publisher abstract reports contact of a chiral crystal with an initially achiral crystal and a subsequent chirality-transfer transformation. The native SI establishes movie identities and documents associated optical microscopy and thermal estimates. The main paper remains inaccessible, so its full experimental and analytical controls have not been verified. [Acquisition and checks](<ACQUISITION.md>) distinguish these evidence classes.

In addition to the saved HTTP 403 response, root inspected the normal browser's native ACS article page and observed explicit purchase/access-restriction notices while abstract and SI remained available. That primary UI check is recorded in ACQUISITION.md; it does not supply the missing main methods or invalidate the freely acquired supplement.

## What was observed

- PDF page 11, SI S4, identifies Movie SI7 as transformation triggered when a second creeping crystal contacts a crystal at the laser focus. It identifies Movie SI8 as directed contact-induced chirality transfer. Both captions specify real-time playback.
- Movie SI8 contact sheet (dependency not included: `documented-scaffold-2026-10-18/contact-chirality/sources/movie8-contact-sheet.png`) shows two distinguishable crystal bodies, development of a bridge near the marked laser focus, connection, and a visual change in the right-hand body. The author assigns this to chirality transfer. Image appearance alone does not independently determine the sign or magnitude of optical rotation.
- Movie SI7 contact sheet (dependency not included: `documented-scaffold-2026-10-18/contact-chirality/sources/movie7-contact-sheet.png`) shows the contact/transformation geometry at a smaller scale. This is a separate movie and is not an independent serial generation.
- SI S2, PDF pages 5–8, discusses the focal crystal and a separately creeping crystal; Figure S2 marks the different bodies. Its thermal treatment assumes a focal region at 200 °C and contact material at 48 °C, with the latter based on an estimated temperature profile. These are model assumptions and estimates, not measurements of the temperature of each crystal during transfer.
- SI S1, PDF pages 2–4, describes pre-crystallization changes in refractive appearance and possible concentration/thermal effects. The authors explicitly leave the origin unresolved. It cannot be used as a measured chemical copying rate.

The PDF, movies and their source captions are preserved. We did not digitize outcomes or infer transfer rates, energies, fidelity, kinetics or sample counts from these artifacts.

Visual statements concern the one-frame-per-second contact sheets, not exhaustive inspection of every native frame. Absence of donor removal in those samples does not prove that an event could not occur between sampled frames or elsewhere in the inaccessible main article. The acquired captions do not document a subsequent isolated-recipient functional assay.

## Inheritance and function gates

| Requirement | Evidence in the acquired material | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Initially separate recipient | Two bodies visible in Movie SI8; main abstract describes initially achiral recipient | Supported as a source-reported spatial/state distinction, with main assay details unverified |
| New material rather than retained parent | Transformation concerns recipient material already present before contact; no isotopic/source mass tracing | Recipient state change is plausible; no quantitative founder upper bound |
| Parent-state causal direction | Publisher claims chiral-to-achiral contact transfer | Exact L/R donor labels, reciprocal trials and no-contact controls not verified in acquired SI |
| Optical function in recipient | Qualitative microscopy visible; no recipient-resolved optical-rotation calibration or polarizer-angle series in acquired SI | Unresolved |
| Persistence after donor removal | Neither inspected sheet shows donor removal followed by recipient-function measurement | Not demonstrated by acquired observations |
| Functional progeny beyond founder activity | No isolated recipient assay or measured founder contribution ceiling | Unresolved |
| Serial transmission | No successive isolated recipient-to-recipient generations or lineage table | Unresolved |
| Heritable variants under common feed | No reciprocal donor-state × common-feed functional matrix or copying-error distribution | Unresolved |
| Noncarbon organization | NaClO3 and Au are inorganic; the acquired methods do not invoke an organic chiral driver | No organic state cue identified, but inaccessible full methods prevent a complete materials/fabrication inventory |
| Shared environment with target scaffold | Local laser heating, solvent evaporation and engineered substrate control the experiment | No compatibility test with Mo34WV or PMo12 |

Contact-induced conversion of pre-existing recipient matter could be a valuable physical state-copying mechanism. It need not reproduce the donor's mass to convey a state. The full goal still requires that a recipient carry a measured function, retain it independently, and pass it serially under conditions that separate donor-state influence from apparatus bias, background nucleation and residual donor fragments. These observations do not supply that chain.

The imaging does not justify treating NaClO3 growth as autonomous replication or chiral sign as general functional genetic information. No active repair, metabolism, adaptive evolution or cognition is demonstrated. External optical energy and apparatus are counted as dependencies rather than silently attributed to the material.

## Model decision and preserved negatives

No new inheritance fit is warranted from this package. There is no numerical repeated-transition table or calibrated recipient-function response in the acquired SI. A model assigning a copying probability from a selected movie would insert the desired answer. Quantitative source-local modeling should wait for verified main methods, replicated opposite-handed donor controls, and isolated recipient optical measurements or another measured function.

Specific unresolved items retained: main article access; handedness calibration; reciprocal controls; donor exclusion; retained function after separation; serial generations; common-feed variants; complete carbon inventory; integration with the target; and a public raw quantitative inheritance dataset. The 2023 polarimetry lead remains unacquired and on-request, as recorded in ACQUISITION.md. Existing 2021/2019 chlorate negatives are preserved and are not replaced by this contact-transformation case.
