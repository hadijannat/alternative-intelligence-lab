## Input/response independence and unresolved observation mapping

Fig. 6 is described as dissolved Fe/Ni amount versus sampling time, with electrolyte concentration on a second axis. The amount and concentration axes represent converted versions of one ICP-MS measurement; they are not two independent observations. Panel 6c explicitly repeats the NiFe data from 6a, so those curves must not be counted as replicates. **SI Fig. S9 shows average beginning/end operating potentials**, not a continuous six-hour potential trace. SI Fig. S7 provides the analogous LDH accumulation plots. The acquired files offer plotted measurements, not raw instrument exports.

Main Fig. 1 (`sources/fig1-methods.png`) shows the same right-hand KOH reservoir feeding the SFC in both states. The outlet returns to that reservoir during recirculation and goes to ICP-MS during sampling; the separate baseline reservoir feeds only the analyzer in the recirculation state. There is no depicted fresh-electrolyte replacement or complete reset. SI Scheme 1 (`sources/si-media/image1.png`) supplies cell geometry and the IS Y-connector but no reservoir volume or sampling correction. Thus a retained reservoir is supported topologically, while exact retained volume, replacement and concentration-to-total-loss bookkeeping remain unverified.

A usable quantitative dataset would pair independently imposed material, alkalinity, current/potential program, flow state and sample time with concentration readings and corresponding electrode potential. Calibrated inference of dissolution kinetics also needs reservoir/loop volumes, valve timing, sampled volume, replacement solution, mass-conversion formula, blanks and censoring. Neither a cumulative constant-loss law nor a complete interval reset follows uniquely from the acquired methods. In particular, multiplying concentration by the quoted total electrolyte volume would impose an observation model that should be labeled and tested explicitly.

No source outcomes were numerically extracted. Source searching exposed the auditor to qualitative outcomes and rounded endpoint statements in surrounding text, so later evaluation must be labeled retrospective and paper-informed. Native plotted responses may be extracted under a separately frozen protocol, with pixel and observation-model uncertainty. Search-rendered rounded endpoints are insufficient substitutes for that extraction.

## Native axis and equation checks

The Fig. 6 crop is `sources/fig6-axis-audit.png`. Inspection of its **axis ticks only**, followed by extraction of black major-tick rectangles in panel 6a, gives an approximate graphical conversion of **1.617 µg/cm² per ppb** (`sources/fig6-axis-calibration.json`). Interpreting ppb as µg/L and using exactly 50 mL with 0.02835 cm² would instead give 1.764 µg/cm² per ppb. The graphical ratio is about 8.3% smaller. Its conditional effective volume is approximately 45.8 mL; this is **not a measured reservoir volume or uncertainty estimate**. Graphical precision, the source's approximate volume, sampling losses, or other calibration choices could matter; the source does not establish which explains the difference. The tick audit therefore rules out treating the approximate volume as an exact validated conversion and does not establish a thousand-fold mL/µL typo. Response curves were visible during this axis audit but no response points were extracted.

The initial plain-text extraction misleadingly concatenated the Eq. S5 fraction into `0.0593`. Inspection of native DOCX mathematical XML **rejects that apparent Nernst-coefficient error**: zero-based `m:oMath` element 10 contains a fraction with numerator `0.059` and denominator `3`. Exact equation subtrees are saved as `sources/si-equation-09.xml`, `-10.xml`, `-12.xml`, and `-13.xml`, with structural summaries in `si-math-structure.json`. Eq. S5's ferrate/FeOOH half-reaction includes three electrons and its coefficient retains the division by three.

A separate displayed Reaction 30 really does encode `FeO4(2−) + 8H(+) + 3e(−) ⇋ Fe(3+) + 5OH(−)` in native element 12. That expression fails atom and charge balance as written. Preserve it as a source transcription inconsistency; it does **not** establish that Eq. S5 or the computed Pourbaix diagram is wrong. Do not repair the deposited source silently or propagate that reaction into a chemical mechanism.

## Meaning for repair and inheritance

The authors interpret sharp transient signals as particle detachment and sustained Fe accumulation as net ionic loss. These distinctions are hypotheses supported by the reported experimental configuration; no acquired particle-resolved chemical speciation or isotope-origin functional assay establishes them universally. The acidic IS mixing occurs before analysis and should not be assumed to preserve the incoming solid/ionic state. ICP-MS elemental mass alone does not identify an active catalytic site, and naturally abundant isotope channels are not origin labeling.

The retrieved methods do not establish matched damage followed by metal-resupply/withdrawal arms, independent founder-capacity bounds, selective removal of founders, origin-resolved new-site function, or serial functional descendants. Any explanation involving simultaneous dissolution and redeposition remains compatible with unchanged total current and mixed old/new contributions. Thus the source can constrain an eventual **loss-observation model** but presently cannot identify active repair or close functional inheritance, intelligent behavior, or compatibility with the hypothetical Mo34WV cluster.

## Decision

**The current evidence supports a frozen synthetic sampling/mass-balance test; calibrated physical repair-rate inference remains unsupported.** The source constrains material, nominal flow, sampling times and apparatus topology, while retaining collection and calibration ambiguity. Do not fit source response curves in this stage. A future retrospective measurement test would require a separately frozen extraction protocol and explicit collection assumptions. Neither type of observation test identifies active repair or functional inheritance without the missing origin/function controls.
