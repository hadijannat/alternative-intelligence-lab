# Organization-level renewal and atom-level attribution

The original R contract requires new competent material to restore measured function after a specified perturbation, while feed and founders are accounted. It also requires rivals including passive survival, altered founders, externally supplied competent catalyst, no regeneration and matched inputs. These requirements are retained.

Exclusive assignment of turnover to incoming atoms is a stronger question. A reconstructed active assembly can contain retained substrate atoms and newly supplied matter. A valid causal/material experiment may establish restoration of that assembly without allocating every catalytic event among its atoms. Isotope tracing and isolated-new-material assays are useful sufficient routes; neither is a universal requirement for every component R demonstration.

The prior `origin-function-followup/PROTOCOL.md` explicitly concerns origin-resolved functional attribution. Its frozen SHA256 `45b71ca66ad444a71586a53bb6174fd51fc3c9d81eb56b58e72314ae904908b2` and the four-pool non-identifiability results are unchanged. They must not be generalized into a proof that organization-level repair is unidentifiable under every intervention. No previous source is promoted by this clarification.

One sufficient experimental route combines independently verified damage to the claimed competent assembly; damaged/sham x precursor/vehicle conditions; common-medium post-assays; measured reconstitution from accounted material; and a selective necessity test or another validated discriminator of the rebuilt assembly. Matched deblocking/redox-reactivation and ready-made-catalyst controls address different rivals. A positive factorial interaction alone cannot supply the missing material evidence.

The newly acquired Nishioka source shows why the distinction matters. It documents common-medium functional recovery after an external KOH oxidation program and also reports phosphate-associated material loss. It cannot safely be classified as a known pure-deblocking negative. Yet it does not resolve recovered function among retained exposed sites, substrate-derived newly formed active material and recaptured dissolved material. This is a component recovery observation with unresolved renewal attribution.

All twelve integrated requirements remain unclosed. Even a future successful source-level R experiment would need separate compatibility, balance and same-system evidence for J, founder-bounded descendants for H/V and causal adaptation/generalization for A/C.
