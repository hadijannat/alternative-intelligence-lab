# GTM-3 follow-up: useful chirality still lacks a descendant-function measurement

Checked 2026-10-18. Scope: three direct follow-up sources to the previously acquired JACS 2022 GTM-3 article, whose package remains unchanged in `../functional-carrier-search/sources/gtm3/`. No author contact, kinetic fitting, or new chemical simulation was performed.

## Decision

No crossed-seed offspring catalytic-function matrix, cue-free serial propagation experiment, or founder-excluded descendant catalytic measurement was located in the accessible follow-up material. This is an observation about this bounded source set, not proof that inorganic functional inheritance is impossible. GTM-3 remains a documented functional-state carrier; it is not qualified as an inheritance complement to PMo12 or the hypothetical Mo34WV target.

The new sources strengthen the structural assignment and identify a practical achiral activation/stabilization complement. They also preserve a high-temperature inactivation control that a purely beneficial activation model would miss.

## Primary-source assessment

### 1. Catalysis Today 426 (2024), 114389

[DOI 10.1016/j.cattod.2023.114389](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cattod.2023.114389), *Asymmetric catalysis within chiral zeolitic nanospaces: Chiral host-guest match in GTM-3 zeolite*.

The publisher's indexed abstract reports alcohol-size, epoxide-concentration and temperature effects, with product ee up to 51% and opposite behavior for catalysts prepared using opposite SDA enantiomers. These are reaction-condition comparisons. The indexed Data availability statement is: “Data will be made available on request.” No open numerical descendant-function dataset was linked in the accessible indexed content.

Access limitation: both the web reader and urllib returned 403. The saved `sources/gtm3/cattod-publisher-response.html` contains a ScienceDirect error page, with `CPE00001` and `CLOUDFLARE_ERROR_1000S_BOX`. It is an access-failure artifact, not acquired article text. Full text/SI could not be audited here; absence of an inheritance measurement cannot be asserted for inaccessible sections. Indexed abstract and availability observations came from the publisher search result for the exact DOI on the checked date.

### 2. ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 16 (2024), 54067-54080

[DOI 10.1021/acsami.4c14487](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.4c14487), *On the Origin of Enantioselectivity in Chiral Zeolite Asymmetric Catalyst GTM-3: Host-Guest Transfer of Chirality*.

Full XML and 14-page SI acquired through Europe PMC. Section 3.5 and Table 1 resolve crystal handedness: (+)-GTM-3 has 13 P4332 and 2 P4132 crystals; (-)-GTM-3 has 10 P4132 and 3 P4332 crystals. The authors' approximately 82% enrichment is a majority-handedness fraction, not an 82% crystal ee. SI Tables S2-S3 provide individual refinement results.

Section 2.1 explicitly uses opposite enantiopure EMPS SDAs for the two preparations. Section 2.2/Figure 1 links those preparations to opposite catalytic product selectivity after calcination. These are independently prepared catalysts. No crossed-seed offspring function, serial generations, achiral growth cue, or founder exclusion appears in the acquired article/SI. The source improves identity/function linkage; it does not estimate inheritance fidelity. Calcined catalysts were handled in dry nitrogen to prevent framework degradation.

Exact local anchors: `2024-body.txt` lines 7, 9, 15, 65 and 89; XML Section 3.5/Table 1; `2024-supporting-information.txt` pages 5-6, Tables S2-S3.

### 3. JACS 147 (2025), 38585-38598

[DOI 10.1021/jacs.5c12567](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c12567), *Enzyme-like Enantioselectivity in GTM Chiral Zeolite Catalysts upon Preactivation of Ge Sites*.

Full XML and 15-page SI were acquired. The primary article reports that achiral 1-butanol preactivation improves GTM-3 unlike-product ee from -27% to -64% at low conversion (SI Figure S1). GTM-4 reaches -78% under ambient-temperature reaction conditions and -88% at -20 C; those stronger values must not be assigned to GTM-3. The GTM-4 humidity study reports preserved crystallinity after 168 h ambient-air exposure following butanol loading, versus immediate collapse without that protection.

Negative control: preactivation at 120 C creates irreversibly bound butoxide and inactive catalysts. Thus activation and stabilization depend on chemical state and treatment conditions. These findings support an achiral, carbon-containing local complement for existing inorganic catalysts. They do not show cue-free production of new chiral catalysts, and ambient-humidity resistance does not establish aqueous POM compatibility. Section 2.1 still specifies chiral SDA synthesis from organic alkaloids.

Exact local anchors: `2025-body.txt` lines 5, 25, 29, 31, 67 and 71; article Figures 1-2 and Discussion; linked SI Figures S1, S4-S7. Numerical values here are author-reported comparisons, not newly digitized trajectories.

The 14-page main PDF was subsequently acquired from the institutional UCL `/id/eprint/10221401/3/` route. `2025-main-page3.png` contains Figure 1 (journal page 38587), and `2025-main-page4.png` contains Figure 2. Figure 1 and SI S1 were visually inspected; no points were digitized. Figure 1A-C uses conversion as its horizontal axis. Unlike products, like products, remaining reactants and secondary products have distinct distributions: a single-product kinetic-resolution balance cannot be assumed to cover all converted substrate. The reported 100% ee of remaining substrate is also subject to the unreported analytical detection limit and rounding; ee is undefined when remaining substrate is exactly zero.

Condition audit for downstream modeling:

- Catalysts: calcination at 200 C for 10 h under ozone-enriched oxygen; catalytic preactivation in neat 1-butanol at room temperature overnight. The assay uses 20 wt% catalyst relative to TSO, 1 mg/mL racemic TSO in 1-butanol and room-temperature stirring unless specified otherwise.
- Figure S1 (rendered in `2025-si-page5.png`) plots ee against conversion, not elapsed time. The untreated GTM-3 curve changes from approximately -12% to -48% with conversion; the textual -27% to -64% comparison is a low-conversion comparison, not an exact matched-time observation. Do not infer adsorption or catalytic rate constants from it.
- The humidity assay is GTM-4(RS-OMBMEP), with 50 C butanol loading and XRD at 24, 96 and 168 h in air; SI S7 repeats the exposure sequence after 115 C loading. Relative humidity and a loading duration were not located alongside those conditions. This is crystallinity survival, not timed catalytic activity survival.
- The 120 C inactivation statement has no linked numerical conversion, detection limit, or assay duration. Treat it as an author-reported qualitative negative, not a calibrated zero rate.
- A prespecified stoichiometric kinetic-resolution rule could be evaluated against unfitted conversion/selectivity conditions. An activation-rate model cannot be calibrated from these ee-versus-conversion figures. All cited conditions have now been inspected; any split is retrospective rather than newly unseen validation. Separate variant comparisons are not serial descendants.

Carbon roles are explicit: chiral organic SDAs establish initial handedness; achiral butanol is a coordinating ligand, solvent and nucleophile; organic TSO is the assay substrate. This is an inorganic framework with organic complements, not a carbon-free working system.
